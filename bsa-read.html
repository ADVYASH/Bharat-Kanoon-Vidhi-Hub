CHAPTER I

Preliminary

Sections 1–2

Introduces the short title, application and commencement of the Act, together with definitions and explanations of important terms used in the law of evidence.

CHAPTER II

Of the Relevancy of Facts

Sections 3–50

Covers facts in issue, relevant facts, connected transactions, admissions, confessions, statements by persons who cannot be called as witnesses, judgments, expert opinions and character when relevant.

CHAPTER III

Facts Which Need Not Be Proved

Sections 51–53

Explains facts that do not require formal proof, including facts subject to judicial notice and facts admitted by the parties, as provided by the Act.

CHAPTER IV

Of Oral Evidence

Sections 54–55

Deals with proof of facts by oral evidence and the general requirement that oral evidence must be direct, subject to the applicable provisions of the Act.

CHAPTER V

Of Documentary Evidence

Sections 56–93

Covers proof of document contents, primary and secondary evidence, electronic and digital records, public and private documents, presumptions relating to documents and proof of signatures and records.

CHAPTER VI

Of the Exclusion of Oral Evidence by Documentary Evidence

Sections 94–103

Explains when documentary terms must be proved through the document itself, how oral evidence may be restricted by documentary evidence and the circumstances in which exceptions or supplementary evidence may be permitted.

CHAPTER VII

Of the Burden of Proof

Sections 104–120

Addresses the burden of proof, the obligation to prove particular facts, evidentiary presumptions, facts especially within a person's knowledge and related rules governing proof in judicial proceedings.

CHAPTER VIII

Estoppel

Sections 121–123

Covers the principle of estoppel and specified circumstances involving tenants, licensees, acceptors of bills and bailees who may be prevented from denying particular facts or legal relationships.

CHAPTER IX

Of Witnesses

Sections 124–139

Addresses competency to testify, privileges, communications protected by law, professional communications, accomplice evidence and the examination of witnesses in specified circumstances.

CHAPTER X

Of Examination of Witnesses

Sections 140–168

Covers the order of examination, examination-in-chief, cross-examination, re-examination, leading questions, refreshing memory, questions affecting credibility, corroboration, improper questions and related rules for presenting witness testimony.

CHAPTER XI

Of Improper Admission and Rejection of Evidence

Section 169

Addresses the effect of improper admission or rejection of evidence on a decision, including whether the outcome would have been different without the evidentiary error.

CHAPTER XII

Repeal and Savings

Section 170

Provides for repeal of the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, and savings relating to proceedings or matters covered by the statutory transition provisions.
