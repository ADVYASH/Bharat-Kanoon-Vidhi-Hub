About IPC

Understanding India's former substantive criminal law legislation.

The Indian Penal Code, 1860 (IPC) was India's principal substantive criminal law statute. It defined numerous criminal offences and prescribed punishments for conduct covered by its provisions.

The IPC was enacted in 1860 and came into force on 1 January 1862. It addressed criminal responsibility, punishments, general exceptions, abetment, offences against the human body, property offences and numerous other categories of criminal conduct.

The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS), Act No. 45 of 2023, replaced the IPC with effect from 1 July 2024, subject to applicable repeal, savings and transitional provisions. Certain earlier matters may continue to be governed by the applicable savings provisions.

Why Study the IPC?

The IPC played a central role in the development of criminal law in India. Its provisions and judicial interpretation remain important for historical legal research and for understanding cases governed by applicable earlier law.

Studying the IPC is useful for law students, advocates, researchers and readers examining the evolution of criminal offences, punishments and legal principles.

The IPC should be considered alongside the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS), the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS), and the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA). Which legislation applies to a particular matter depends on the relevant legal provisions, dates and transitional rules.

Legal Resources

Explore former and current criminal law legislation.

Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) - 2023 The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, defines criminal offences and prescribes punishments under India's substantive criminal law framework. It addresses offences against individuals, property, public order, and the state, replacing the Indian Penal Code, 1860, subject to applicable legal provisions. Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS) - 2023 The Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, establishes the procedural framework for criminal justice in India. It governs police investigations, arrests, bail, trials, inquiries, appeals, and related judicial processes, replacing the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, subject to applicable legal provisions. Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) - 2023 The Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, establishes the legal framework governing evidence in judicial proceedings. It addresses the relevancy and admissibility of facts, documentary evidence, electronic records, witness testimony, and proof of facts, replacing the Indian Evidence Act, 1872, subject to applicable legal provisions. Indian Penal Code (IPC) - 1860 The Indian Penal Code, 1860, was India's principal substantive criminal law legislation, defining numerous offences and prescribing their punishments. It covered offences involving the human body, property, public tranquillity, and other matters. It was replaced by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, subject to applicable transitional provisions. Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) - 1973 The Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973, established the procedural framework for administering criminal justice in India. It governed investigations, arrests, bail, inquiries, trials, appeals, and revisions. It was replaced by the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, subject to applicable transitional provisions. Indian Evidence Act (IEA) - 1872 The Indian Evidence Act, 1872, established the legal framework governing the admissibility and relevancy of evidence in Indian judicial proceedings. It addressed facts in issue, documentary evidence, witness examination, presumptions, and proof of facts. It was replaced by the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023, subject to applicable transitional provisions.
