CHAPTER I

Preliminary

Sections 1–4

Introduces the short title, application and commencement of the Act, together with definitions and explanations of important terms used in the law of evidence.

CHAPTER II

Of the Relevancy of Facts

Sections 5–55

Covers facts in issue, relevant facts, connected transactions, admissions, confessions, statements by persons who cannot be called as witnesses, judgments, expert opinions and character when relevant.

CHAPTER III

Facts Which Need Not Be Proved

Sections 56–58

Explains facts that do not require formal proof, including facts subject to judicial notice and facts admitted by the parties, as provided by the Act.

CHAPTER IV

Of Oral Evidence

Sections 59–60

Deals with proof of facts by oral evidence and the general requirement that oral evidence must be direct, subject to the applicable provisions of the Act.

CHAPTER V

Of Documentary Evidence

Sections 61–90

Covers proof of document contents, primary and secondary evidence, public and private documents, presumptions concerning documents and proof of signatures and records.

CHAPTER VI

Of the Exclusion of Oral Evidence by Documentary Evidence

Sections 91–100

Explains when documentary terms must be proved through the document itself, how oral evidence may be restricted by documentary evidence and the circumstances in which exceptions apply.

CHAPTER VII

Of the Burden of Proof

Sections 101–114A

Addresses the burden of proof, the obligation to prove particular facts, presumptions, facts especially within a person's knowledge and statutory presumptions in specified cases.

CHAPTER VIII

Estoppel

Sections 115–117

Covers estoppel and specified circumstances involving tenants, licensees, acceptors of bills and bailees who may be prevented from denying particular facts or legal relationships.

CHAPTER IX

Of Witnesses

Sections 118–134

Addresses competency to testify, privileged communications, professional communications, accomplice evidence and the general rule concerning the number of witnesses required to prove a fact.

CHAPTER X

Of the Examination of Witnesses

Sections 135–166

Covers the order of examination, examination-in-chief, cross-examination, re-examination, leading questions, refreshing memory, credibility, corroboration, improper questions and judicial control of evidence.

CHAPTER XI

Of Improper Admission and Rejection of Evidence

Section 167

Addresses the effect of improper admission or rejection of evidence on a decision, including whether the outcome would have been different without the evidentiary error.
